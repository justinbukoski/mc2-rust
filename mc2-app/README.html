mc2-app

The mc2 binary and its headless twin: window / offscreen render loop, game states, the fixed-step live simulation (LiveSim over mc2-sim), the camera, order input and the camera visibility producer. Headless runs (mc2 --headless, HeadlessRun) never open a window. --hires-dir DIR (or $MC2_HIRES_DIR) loads an upscaled texture pack; default off. Missing or corrupt pack entries keep the original texture and log a line naming the file. The production app does not read a hires_dir = ... settings file.

Object visibility

Before each live Sim::step, LiveSim runs R1's production select_shapes on its bound mover, building, turret, gate and terrain object appearances for that frame's camera. The resulting inView and retained LOD values are the sim's Visibility input; the headless trace records object-visibility=production. tests/sim_replay.rs compares 60 mc2_01 turns with the C++ oracle: at most 32 selection boxes per class may differ on a turn during camera-edge or intro-frame selection, and the non-iv B/V digest fields must remain identical. The app-to-direct-sim replay is bit-identical. Terrain-vertex visibility remains exact on turns 2..60; turn 1 has the separate 16-vertex clip-edge bound in docs/DIFFERENCES.md.

The mission HUD

With the live simulation the mission draws mc2-ui's composed HUD over the scene (src/hud.rs, src/hud_data.rs): unit bar, info window, tactical map, control panel, objectives, pause window and the mission message log, all built every frame from the simulation's state. Selection, tactical-map and unit-bar camera moves and pause (which stops the simulation's objects, as Mission::update does while paused) are applied by the app. Player orders go through one hook, hud::apply_order_intents, which reports them unavailable until the orders bridge (feat/rust-a3-orders) is merged. What the HUD cannot show yet is listed in hud_data::UNAVAILABLE and in the headless report's sim_unavailable. Evidence: tests/hud_live.rs and docs/mc2_01_live_hud_1920x1080.png.

Scripted timeline

An input script's frames are mission simulation frames. Events, resizes and screenshots are delivered at their sim frame at any render rate (GameCore::scheduled_events, scheduled_resizes, capture_frames).
